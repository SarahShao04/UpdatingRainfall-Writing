\noindent This is the supplementary material for \paperauthorsprose\ (``\papertitle,'' \the\year), which replicates and extends \citet{bruckner2011rain} for 41 Sub-Saharan African (SSA) countries, extending their original 1981--2004 panel to 2024. The material is organized in five appendices. 

\ref{Appendix_A} presents the full replication and extension of every table in \citet{bruckner2011rain} (Tables III--IX). Each table was estimated first on the original 1981--2004 sample to confirm reproduction of the published estimates and then on the sample extended to 2024. A three-sample robustness check is used to isolate the source of any change. 
\ref{Appendix_B} traces how the three core variables --- democracy, real GDP per capita, and rainfall --- were built from their original sources, including the corrections applied through the process. It then checks each reconstructed series against \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s original data over the 1981--2004 overlap.
\ref{Appendix_C} defines every variable, reports descriptive statistics, and lists the full country sample. \ref{Appendix_D} works through why the paper's instrument --- the log of rainfall lagged one period --- is strong and exogenous before 2004 but not after this period. It reports relevance and exogeneity tests, Anderson--Rubin confidence sets, a range of alternative rainfall instruments, and a formal dating of the break to 2007 that survives even when the underlying data vintages are not spliced.

Finally, \ref{Appendix_E} traces the mechanism behind the one relationship that strengthens under extension: rainfall's direct effect on democratic transitions. It decomposes this effect into its country/year drivers and tests four candidate transmission channels.

\section{Full Extended-Sample Replication Tables\label{Appendix_A}}

This appendix reports our replication and extension of Tables III--IX of \citet{bruckner2011rain}. For every table, Panel A reproduces the original specification on the original 1981--2004 sample, verifying our reconstruction of the data and code against the published results. Panel B repeats the identical specification on the sample extended to 2024. All specifications include country fixed effects, country-specific linear time trends, year fixed effects, and cluster standard errors at the country level, exactly as in \citet{bruckner2011rain}. For the instrumental-variables tables (Tables A-3, A-4, and A-7) the instrument is always \textit{Log rainfall, $t-1$}. We report both the conventional 2SLS standard error and the Anderson--Rubin \citep{andersonrubin1949} $p$-value, which is robust to weak instruments. The Anderson--Rubin $p$-value is the more reliable guide to significance whenever \ref{Appendix_D} indicates the first stage is not strong by conventional standards.

\section{Data Construction, Corrections, and Validation\label{Appendix_B}}

This appendix documents how the three core variables --- democracy, real GDP per capita, and rainfall --- were extended beyond \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s (\citeyear{bruckner2011rain}) original 1981--2004 coverage, the data-processing errors that surfaced along the way, and a validation exercise that reconstructs each variable's own 1981--2004 values from our extension pipeline and compares them, year by year, against \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s original data.

\subsection*{Appendix B.1\quad Democracy: Polity V and Rescaled V-Dem}

Democracy is extended in two stages. From 2005--2018 we use Polity V \citep{marshall2020polity5} directly, on the same $-10$ to $+10$ Polity2 scale as the original data; from 2019--2024, following the discontinuation of new Polity releases, we splice in the V-Dem dataset \citep{coppedge2024vdem}, rescaled onto the Polity2 metric. The rescaling maps each Polity component to its closest V-Dem analogue (Polity2 to \texttt{v2x\_polyarchy}, the Electoral Democracy Index; Exconst to \texttt{v2xlg\_legcon}, legislative constraints on the executive; Polcomp and Exrec to \texttt{v2xel\_frefair}, the free-and-fair-elections index), then matches the standard deviation of year-on-year \emph{changes} in each V-Dem series to the standard deviation of year-on-year changes in the corresponding Polity series over the 1982--2018 overlap, and cumulates the rescaled changes forward from each country's last available Polity value. For Polity2 the scaling factor is $\sigma(\Delta\text{Polity2})/\sigma(\Delta v2x\_polyarchy) = 1.8708/0.0445 = 42.08$; corresponding factors for Exconst, Polcomp, and Exrec are 59.77, 49.58, and 17.93. All rescaled series are clipped to their native Polity ranges ($-10$ to 10 for Polity2; 1--7, 1--10, and 1--8 for the concept scores).

\subsection*{Appendix B.2\quad Real GDP per Capita: Penn World Table 11.0}

Real GDP per capita is extended for 2005--2023 using Penn World Table (PWT) 11.0 \citep{feenstra2015pwt}, constructed as $\text{lgdp} = \log(\text{rgdpe}/\text{pop})$, where \texttt{rgdpe} is expenditure-side real GDP and \texttt{pop} is population, both from PWT's \texttt{Data} sheet. PWT 11.0 reports levels in 2021 constant PPP dollars, whereas the original \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain} data use PWT 6.2's 2005 USD base; within-version growth rates are unaffected by this choice, but the \emph{level} of GDP per capita is not directly comparable across the 2004/2005 splice point, a fact we return to in Appendix B.4 and use as the basis for the vintage-splice robustness check in \ref{Appendix_D}. Real GDP per capita is not available past 2023 in the current PWT release, so \texttt{lgdp} for 2024 is missing by construction; its one- and two-period lags are still available for all 1981--2024 country-years.

\subsection*{Appendix B.3\quad Rainfall: GPCP v2.3 and the Longitude-Convention Bug}

Rainfall is extended for 2005--2024 using the NASA/NOAA Global Precipitation Climatology Project (GPCP) Monthly Precipitation Climate Data Record, version 2.3 \citep{huffman1997gpcp}, on a $2.5^{\circ}\times2.5^{\circ}$ grid reported in mm/day. Each monthly grid is converted to a monthly total (multiplying by days in month), summed to an annual total, and spatially joined to country polygons from the Natural Earth 1:50m Admin-0 boundaries; small countries whose land area falls between grid cells are assigned their nearest grid-cell centroid rather than an empty spatial average.

While building this pipeline we discovered and corrected two bugs that had silently zeroed out or misattributed rainfall for a subset of countries in earlier drafts of this project. Table B-2 documents both, the tables and figures they affected, and the quantified effect of the fix; both corrections are reflected in every result reported in this paper and its supplemental materials.

\subsection*{Appendix B.4\quad Validation Against the Original Data}

To check that the extension pipeline reproduces \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s own values rather than merely being internally consistent, we rebuild each of the three variables' 1981--2004 series independently, using exactly the extension-period methodology, and compare the result to the original data year by year. Figures B-1--B-3 and Tables B-4--B-6 report the comparison; the underlying code is in \texttt{data\_validation.ipynb}.

\textbf{Rainfall.} The reconstructed GPCP v2.3 series tracks the original GPCP v2.0/2.1 series closely from 1981--1990 (within $\pm5\%$, with no consistent direction), then diverges systematically upward from 1991 onward, peaking at roughly $+16\%$ around 2001 and narrowing to $+9.9\%$ by 2004 (Table B-4; median absolute difference over the full overlap is 6.2\%, mean 7.3\%). This pattern matches \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s own documented caveat that GPCP's underlying gauge density fell from roughly 32 stations per country in the 1980s to 18 in the 1990s and 8 after 2000, and is consistent with GPCP v2.3 being a reprocessed climate data record that incorporates gauge stations recovered after the original v2.0/2.1 release --- a genuine data revision by the provider, not a defect in our aggregation, which we verified directly against the raw NetCDF files.

Table A-8 shows that the direct, reduced-form relationship between rainfall and democratic transitions is the one result in this paper that \emph{strengthens} under extension, from $-0.125^{**}$ to $-0.142^{***}$, even as the 2SLS income-shock channel weakens. This appendix asks what is driving that relationship: which country-years account for it, whether it survives excluding any single country, whether it operates through income at all, and which of several candidate real-economy channels can account for it.
