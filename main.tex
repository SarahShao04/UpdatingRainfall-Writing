\noindent This is the supplementary material for \paperauthorsprose\ (``\papertitle,'' \the\year), which replicates and extends \citet{bruckner2011rain} for 41 Sub-Saharan African (SSA) countries, extending their original 1981--2004 panel to 2024. The material is organized in five appendices. 

\ref{Appendix_A} presents the full replication and extension of every table in \citet{bruckner2011rain} (Tables III--IX). Each table was estimated first on the original 1981--2004 sample to confirm reproduction of the published estimates and then on the sample extended to 2024. A three-sample robustness check is used to isolate the source of any change. 
\ref{Appendix_B} traces how the three core variables --- democracy, real GDP per capita, and rainfall --- were built from their original sources, including the corrections applied through the process. It then checks each reconstructed series against \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s original data over the 1981--2004 overlap.
\ref{Appendix_C} defines every variable, reports descriptive statistics, and lists the full country sample. \ref{Appendix_D} works through why the paper's instrument --- the log of rainfall lagged one period --- is strong and exogenous before 2004 but not after this period. It reports relevance and exogeneity tests, Anderson--Rubin confidence sets, a range of alternative rainfall instruments, and a formal dating of the break to 2007 that survives even when the underlying data vintages are not spliced.

Finally, \ref{Appendix_E} traces the mechanism behind the one relationship that strengthens under extension: rainfall's direct effect on democratic transitions. It decomposes this effect into its country/year drivers and tests four candidate transmission channels.

\section{Full Extended-Sample Replication Tables\label{Appendix_A}}

This appendix reports our replication and extension of Tables III--IX of \citet{bruckner2011rain}. For every table, Panel A reproduces the original specification on the original 1981--2004 sample, verifying our reconstruction of the data and code against the published estimates. Panel B repeats the identical specification on the sample extended to 2024. All specifications include country fixed effects, country-specific linear time trends, year fixed effects, and cluster standard errors at the country level, exactly as in \citet{bruckner2011rain}. For the instrumental-variables tables (Tables A-3, A-4, and A-7) the instrument is always \textit{Log rainfall, $t-1$}. We report both the conventional 2SLS standard error and the Anderson--Rubin \citep{andersonrubin1949} $p$-value, which is robust to weak instruments. The Anderson--Rubin $p$-value is the more reliable guide to significance whenever \ref{Appendix_D} indicates the first stage is not strong by conventional standards.

\section{Data Construction, Corrections, and Validation\label{Appendix_B}}

This appendix documents how the three core variables --- democracy, real GDP per capita, and rainfall --- were extended beyond \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s (\citeyear{bruckner2011rain}) original 1981--2004 coverage and the data-processing errors that surfaced along the way. It closes with a validation exercise that reconstructs each variable's own 1981--2004 values from our extension pipeline and compares them, year by year, against \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s original data.

\subsection*{Appendix B.1\quad Democracy: Polity V and Rescaled V-Dem}

Democracy is extended in two stages. From 2005--2018, we use Polity V \citep{marshall2020polity5} directly, on the same $-10$ to $+10$ Polity2 scale as the original data; from 2019--2024, following the discontinuation of new Polity releases, we splice in the V-Dem dataset \citep{coppedge2024vdem}, rescaled onto the Polity2 metric. The rescaling procedure maps each Polity component to its closest V-Dem analogue: Polity2 to \texttt{v2x\_polyarchy} (the Electoral Democracy Index), Exconst to \texttt{v2xlg\_legcon} (legislative constraints on the executive), and Polcomp and Exrec to \texttt{v2xel\_frefair} (the free-and-fair-elections index). Each V-Dem series is then rescaled so that the standard deviation of its year-on-year changes matches the standard deviation of year-on-year changes in the corresponding Polity series over the 1982--2018 overlap, and the rescaled changes are cumulated forward from each country's last available Polity value. For Polity2, the scaling factor is $\sigma(\Delta\text{Polity2})/\sigma(\Delta v2x\_polyarchy) = 1.8708/0.0445 = 42.08$; corresponding factors for Exconst, Polcomp, and Exrec are 59.77, 49.58, and 17.93. All rescaled series are constrained to their native Polity ranges ($-10$ to 10 for Polity2; 1--7, 1--10, and 1--8 for the concept scores).

Table B-1 reports the correlation between Polity2 and its V-Dem analogue over the full 1981--2018 overlap, which justifies using \texttt{v2x\_polyarchy} as the reference series for rescaling.

\subsection*{Appendix B.2\quad Real GDP per Capita: Penn World Table 11.0}

Real GDP per capita is extended for 2005--2023 using Penn World Table (PWT) 11.0 \citep{feenstra2015pwt}. It is constructed as $\text{lgdp} = \log(\text{rgdpe}/\text{pop})$, where \texttt{rgdpe} is expenditure-side real GDP and \texttt{pop} is population, both taken from PWT's \texttt{Data} sheet. PWT 11.0 reports levels in 2021 constant PPP dollars, whereas the original \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain} data use PWT 6.2's 2005 USD base. Within-version growth rates are unaffected by this choice, but the \emph{level} of GDP per capita is not directly comparable across the 2004/2005 splice point. We return to this fact in Appendix B.4 and use it as the basis for the vintage-splice robustness check in \ref{Appendix_D}. Real GDP per capita is not available past 2023 in the current PWT release, so \texttt{lgdp} for 2024 is missing by construction. Its one- and two-period lags, however, are still available for all 1981--2024 country-years.

\subsection*{Appendix B.3\quad Rainfall: GPCP v2.3 and the Longitude-Convention Bug}

Rainfall is extended for 2005--2024 using the NASA/NOAA Global Precipitation Climatology Project (GPCP) Monthly Precipitation Climate Data Record, version 2.3 \citep{huffman1997gpcp}. This record reports rainfall on a $2.5^{\circ}\times2.5^{\circ}$ grid in mm/day. Each monthly grid is converted to a monthly total (multiplying by days in month) and aggregated to an annual total. The result is spatially joined to country boundaries from the Natural Earth 1:50m Admin-0 dataset. Small countries whose land area falls between grid cells have no grid-cell center within their borders. Therefore, we use the value from the nearest grid cell's center point instead of attempting to average over an empty set of cells.

When building this pipeline, we discovered and corrected two bugs that had silently zeroed out or misattributed rainfall for a subset of countries in earlier drafts of this project. Table B-2 documents both bugs, the tables and figures they affected, and the quantified effect of the fix. Both corrections are reflected in every result reported in this paper and its supplemental materials.

\subsection*{Appendix B.4\quad Validation Against the Original Data}

To check that the extension pipeline reproduces \citeauthor{bruckner2011rain}'s own values, not merely that it is internally consistent, we rebuild each of the three variables' 1981--2004 series independently, using exactly the extension-period methodology. The result is compared to the original data, year by year. Figures B-1--B-3 and Tables B-4--B-6 report the comparison; the underlying code is in \texttt{data\_validation.ipynb}.

\textbf{Rainfall.} The reconstructed GPCP v2.3 series tracks the original GPCP v2.0/2.1 series closely from 1981--1990 (within $\pm5\%$, with no consistent direction). GPCP v2.3 is a reprocessed climate data record that incorporates additional gauge stations and satellite-calibration adjustments made after the original v2.0/2.1 release. \subsection*{Appendix D.7\quad Refined Rainfall Instruments}

We construct three alternative instruments intended to address specific threats to relevance or exogeneity: an ENSO-orthogonalized rainfall series that removes each country's own El Ni\~no/Southern Oscillation loading (to rule out a global-climate confound); an irrigation-adjusted series that down-weights rainfall by each country's irrigated agricultural land share (since irrigated agriculture should respond less to rainfall shocks); and a regional-neighbor rainfall series (a GDP-weighted average of rainfall in other countries in the same region, excluding the country itself).

\section{Mechanism Analysis\label{Appendix_E}}

Table A-8 shows that one relationship in this paper strengthens under extension rather than weakening: the direct, reduced-form effect of rainfall on democratic transitions. The coefficient rises from $-0.125^{**}$ to $-0.142^{***}$, even as the 2SLS income-shock channel weakens over the same period. This appendix asks what is driving that relationship: which country-years account for it, whether it survives excluding any single country, whether it operates through income at all, and which of several candidate real-economy channels can account for it.
